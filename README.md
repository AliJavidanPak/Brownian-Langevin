# Brownian Motion and Langevin Dynamics  A theoretical study of Brownian motion, developed from Einstein's random-walk description through Langevin dynamics and the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process.  The project begins with Robert Brown's observation of the irregular motion of suspended particles and develops Einstein's 1905 theory using a random-walk model. The continuum limit is derived to obtain the Gaussian probability distribution and diffusion equation, followed by Einstein's derivation of the Stokes–Einstein relation.  The second part develops Langevin's dynamical description by introducing viscous damping and stochastic forcing. The Langevin equation is solved explicitly, and the statistical properties of the velocity are derived, including its mean, variance, equilibrium Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution, and velocity autocorrelation function. Matching the equilibrium variance to statistical mechanics yields the fluctuation–dissipation relation.  The analysis is then extended from velocity to position to derive the mean-squared displacement and its two limiting regimes: ballistic motion at short times and diffusion at long times. The crossover occurs on the relaxation timescale $\tau_R=m/\gamma$.  Finally, the Langevin equation is identified with the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process and connected to Markov processes, detailed balance, and the Fokker–Planck equation. The project therefore develops a continuous path from microscopic random collisions to macroscopic diffusion and connects stochastic dynamics with the probabilistic framework of Markov processes.
